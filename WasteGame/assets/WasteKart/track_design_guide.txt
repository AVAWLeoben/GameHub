WasteKart — Track Design Guide

Version 1.0 | Cup 1: Meadow Cup

1. Design philosophy

Every track must be memorable because of its layout, not merely its decoration.

WasteKart is a racing-line drawing game for young children. Players should be able to recognize the intended circuit immediately, trace it comfortably with a finger, and discover different strategies through repeated races.

Our two reference tracks:

Daisy Dash 1.9.1: A winding farm circuit with clear straights, contrasting corners and interesting opportunities to cut across grass.

Clover Crossing 1.9.4: A readable figure-eight with one seamless overpass and underpass, demonstrating that crossings work when their driving directions and heights are visually unambiguous.

These are the standards for all future tracks.

2. Layout requirements

Each track should include:

A distinctive silhouette. It must be recognizable even without any decorations.

One meaningful long straight, ideally incorporating the starting grid and finish line.

A mix of left and right corners: at least one broad sweep, one tighter hairpin and one S-bend or chicane.

A deliberate drawing challenge: a choice between an easy, safe line and a shorter, more difficult one.

One continuous, readable lap: no forks, accidental overlaps or confusing junctions.

Enough space between road sections to keep the course readable and preserve room for finger drawing.

A longer course is preferable to an oval, but length alone does not make a track interesting.

3. Overpasses and underpasses

Bridge crossings are allowed but not mandatory.

Use them only when they improve the circuit's layout or identity.

Rules:

Every crossing must have an unmistakable upper road and lower road.

The bridge should look like a continuation of the normal asphalt, not a separate object.

Use thin guardrails, visible supports and subtle shadows.

The lower road must remain readable beneath the bridge.

Karts and their effects must render at the correct elevation.

Collisions and slipstreaming must not occur between karts on different levels.

Clover Crossing 1.9.4 is the visual reference for future bridges. In particular, preserve the successful appearance through the bridge boards.

4. Artwork — Meadow Cup

Use an original, SNES-inspired, tile-based aesthetic.

Element

Meadow Cup standard

Canvas

960 × 600 pixels, matching the current game

Style

Flat pixel art; approximately 8–16 px repeating details

Palette

Approximately 12–16 principal colors

Road

Neutral grey asphalt, strong contrast against grass

Edges

Short alternating red-and-white curbs

Ground

Two or three shades of green

Props

Orchard trees, fences, hay bales, flowers, farm structures

Bridges

Matching asphalt, slim guardrails, simple supports

Decorations must remain away from the road edge. The player must always be able to distinguish asphalt from off-road terrain.

Different Meadow tracks should use different combinations of props instead of identical background patterns.

5. Racing-line gameplay

The track must reward thoughtful drawing without requiring precision that is unrealistic for a four-year-old.

Road speed factor: 1.0

Grass/off-road factor: 0.55

Keep the road nearly constant in width, approximately 38–45 pixels on either side of its centerline for Meadow Cup.

Provide open space around difficult corners for imperfect drawings.

Offer one or two tempting grass shortcuts, but avoid cuts that make most of the circuit unnecessary.

Place power-up boxes toward the outside of the ideal racing line, creating a small detour decision.

Use four ordered checkpoints in the track metadata to describe intended lap progression. Checkpoint enforcement is a future engine task; storing their positions alone does not prevent cheating.

6. Meadow Cup — track identities

Track

Primary identity

Drawing challenge

1. Daisy Dash

Winding farm circuit

Balance broad sweeps, tight turns and shortcuts

2. Clover Crossing

Figure-eight with a seamless bridge

Navigate alternating corner directions and a single crossover

3. Apple Orchard

Orchard circuit with a prominent U-shaped hairpin and a diagonal S-section

Decide how tightly to cut the hairpin while maintaining a smooth exit

4. Bumblebee Bend

Technical zigzag circuit with two contrasting switchbacks

Find a fluid racing line through repeated left-right direction changes

Apple Orchard proposal: Give it a noticeably asymmetric, elongated silhouette rather than another oval. Use orderly apple-tree rows, small fences and a visually important orchard hairpin. No bridge is necessary.

Bumblebee Bend proposal: Make it the most technical Meadow course, with a long opening straight followed by a pronounced zigzag and two different hairpin shapes. Use flower fields, hay bales and perhaps small bee-themed roadside signs. Avoid crossings unless a specific layout requires one.

These are design briefs, not approved final geometries. Each track should be previewed and tested individually before acceptance.

7. Technical acceptance checklist

Before a new track is accepted:

Silhouette is clearly different from existing tracks

Road is continuous and the racing direction is obvious

Long straight, tight turn and S-section are identifiable

Road image, road mask and centerline JSON match

Four starting-grid positions and a checkered finish line are visible

Power-up positions are reachable

Any overpass renders and behaves correctly above the underpass

One-lap, three-lap and four-player races work

Drawing the track on an iPad feels comfortable

No obvious shortcut makes most of the course irrelevant

Approval process: Design one track → inspect full-size preview → install and test on iPad → refine → approve → proceed to the next.

8. Extending the guide to Cup 2

For Coast Cup and later cups, retain Sections 1–3, 5 and 7 as universal rules.

Change:

Biome and visual palette: beaches, water, islands, docks and coastal vegetation for Cup 2.

Prop vocabulary: replace farm scenery with biome-specific objects.

Circuit difficulty: introduce slightly more demanding corner combinations and narrower roads where appropriate.

Signature features: bridges, coastal chicanes and dockside hairpins where they improve the layout.

Water, cliffs and other hazards should initially remain visual off-road surfaces, unless separate gameplay behavior is explicitly implemented.

Core principle: Every new cup changes the environment and difficulty, but every new track must first succeed as a clear, distinctive drawing challenge.
